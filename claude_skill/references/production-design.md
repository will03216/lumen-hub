# Production Design — the world tells the backstory

Set, props, texture, and period detail are not decoration — they carry
character, status, and psychology before anyone moves. In a single generated
shot you often can't direct an actor for ten seconds, but you *can* load the
frame with a world that does the storytelling. This is high-leverage for
Seedance: dense, specific environment nouns render well and read instantly.

---

## 1. Props & set dressing as story

Objects reveal backstory, class, and state of mind. Specify *meaningful*
objects, not generic ones.

> The apartment shows quiet financial strain and nostalgia: mismatched
> furniture, an outdated TV, walls crowded with faded family photos.

> The office is clinical and dehumanizing: sleek glass and steel, no personal
> items, every object perfectly aligned, no visible cables.

A single hero prop can carry a character — give it material and wear:
`a cracked leather satchel, the strap repaired with wire` says more than "a
bag".

## 2. Texture & wear (the anti-CGI lever)

Specifying texture and imperfection is the fastest way out of the plastic
"AI look". Name the surface and its history:

- `scuffed brushed metal, fingerprints near the handle`
- `raw concrete with water stains, paint peeling at the corners`
- `worn oak with ring-marks from old cups`
- For sci-fi, avoid generic gloss: `scuffed panels, cable bundles, handwritten
  labels on the equipment, small signs of human improvisation`

Texture **contrast** makes a point: `a figure in a pristine white suit moving
through a grimy graffiti-covered tunnel`.

## 3. Period & world-building detail

Period-accurate specifics prime immersion better than any "vintage" label:

> 1970s kitchen: avocado-green fridge, patterned linoleum, nicotine-tinted
> walls, a rotary phone on the wall.

Pick 3–4 concrete period anchors per location; that's enough for the audience
to date and place the world.

## 4. Space as a character with an arc

Across a multi-clip story, let the location change with the drama — track the
state in the continuity ledger ([`continuity-and-editing.md`](continuity-and-editing.md))
and write the *delta* each time:

> The house decays with the marriage: each return adds more peeling paint, a
> dead bulb, creeping mold in the corners.

> City progression: bright open plazas early; narrow cluttered alleys with
> oppressive signage as his anxiety climbs.

## 5. Establishing space

When a scene's geography matters, open on an establishing wide that fixes the
layout, then move closer — the audience holds the map for the tighter shots
that follow. Name 1–2 recurring background anchors so the space stays the same
space across cuts.

---

## Density discipline

Load the world, but keep **one** action and **one** camera move — a rich
static-ish frame with deep set dressing beats a busy frame with five things
moving. Put product/identity/text anchors away from anything transforming, and
protect them with a constraint (`label unchanged`, `signage readable`). For
product work, move light and camera around the object rather than restyling it
([`genre-grammar.md`](genre-grammar.md) → Commercial).
